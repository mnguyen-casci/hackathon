Why the "optimized" strategies cost more here

All three variants correctly diagnosed and fixed the bug (DiscountService summed unit price instead of price×quantity), and all three left mvn test passing. But on this one small task, dumping every file into a single prompt was the cheapest option — not the tool-based strategies research and practice usually recommend.

The reason is turns, not tokens. Naive answers in one shot: no exploration, no back-and-forth. JIT-loading and the context-file variant both spend multiple turns reading files and applying edits, and every turn re-sends the growing conversation. Cache reads are cheap per token, but they're not free, and cache writes (new context introduced mid-session) are billed at a premium. JIT-loading wrote more fresh cache across its turns than context-file did, which is why it ended up the most expensive variant here despite moving fewer total tokens.

This doesn't undo the case for context engineering — it bounds it. Naive dumping scales linearly with codebase size and eventually stops fitting in context at all; a hand-written context file is written once and reused across every future session. The crossover point just isn't visible on a 6-file demo project with a single-shot task.
